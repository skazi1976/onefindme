# Shopping on AliExpress in your own language: why search fails, and what works

*Originally published at [write.as](https://write.as/g5c1v14wk88nm) · OneFindMe article archive · [onefindme.com](https://onefindme.com/)*

If English is not your first language, AliExpress search punishes you twice. First,
the catalog is indexed in English, so your everyday word for a product often matches nothing.
Second, when you translate the word yourself, you usually land on the *dictionary* term, not the
*listing* term — and listings use their own vocabulary ("gua sha" instead of "face massage
stone", "sneakers" instead of "sports shoes").

Here is what actually works, from the least to the most reliable.

**Translate a single noun, not a sentence.** Long translated phrases rank badly on the
marketplace. "wide running shoes" beats "comfortable running shoes for people with wide feet" —
and the product noun has to come first.

**Use the seller's images, not their titles.** Titles are keyword soup written for the algorithm.
The photos, the size chart and the 3-star reviews tell you what you are really buying.

**Let a tool do the translation for you.** [OneFindMe](https://onefindme.com/en/) is a free search
engine built for this exact problem: you type what you want in any of 12 languages — Arabic,
Hebrew, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Polish, Portuguese, Russian, Turkish, Filipino or
English — and it resolves your phrase to the right marketplace query, then ranks the best matches
by price and shipping to your country. It is the difference between guessing the English keyword
and simply describing the thing.

**Always set your country before you compare.** Prices, shipping times and customs all change with
the destination. A deal that looks good in dollars can be a poor one once a 40-day delivery and an
import fee are added.

The short version: the search box is not the problem — the language gap is. Close the gap, and
AliExpress becomes searchable in the language you actually think in.
